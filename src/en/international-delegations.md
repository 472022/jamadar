## The Afghan Primary Teachers' Delegation, 1976

In March 1976, as part of a joint India–Afghanistan initiative, a 22-member delegation of Afghan primary teachers visited India. Full responsibility for their stay in Mumbai (21–25 March 1976) was entrusted to the Maharashtra Rajya Prathmik Shikshak Sangh — and, because the delegation spoke Urdu and Pashto, Jamadar personally took on the role of interpreter throughout their visit.

## The American Delegation, 1976

The same year, in July 1976, an American delegation's visit to Mumbai was likewise organised under Jamadar's full responsibility as General Secretary — described in the album as "a joyful moment in life," made possible by the friendship of American guests.

## Representing Maharashtra nationally

Jamadar's work extended beyond his home state. He was photographed as part of an All India Teachers Federation delegation covered by a Tamil-language newspaper in May 1976, and addressed the Jalgaon Zilla Prathmik Shikshak Sangh's triennial convention that same year.

## NCERT, New Delhi

Jamadar was selected to attend a national training camp organised by the NCERT (National Council of Educational Research and Training) in New Delhi in 1972, and was later felicitated for an especially well-received lecture on "Non-Formal Education" at a subsequent NCERT camp. The family also recalls that his association with NCERT went further — that he served on its Governing Body in New Delhi — though this is not independently documented in the surviving album or press clippings.

## World Brotherhood — a friendship with Japan

Among the more personal pages of the family album is one titled "World Brotherhood" (विश्व-बंधुत्व), preserving a letter from a Japanese visitor, Hirosuke Honoil, written after he returned home:

> "I cannot forget you and India. My heart is filled with pleasant memories of India. When I came back to Japan, I found the earth is only a ship. No Indians, no Japanese — we are the same shipmates."
> — Hirosuke Honoil
